Using a VPN Safely

How a VPN protects your browsing, and what to check before you trust one.

Why it matters

A VPN routes your internet traffic through an encrypted connection before it reaches the open internet, which keeps your browsing hidden from whoever controls the local network — relevant if you're working somewhere your connection may be monitored. It does not make you anonymous, and it does not protect against malware already on your device or a compromised account — it protects one specific leg of the journey.

Choosing a provider

Digital rights organizations that work directly with activists and journalists — including Access Now and groups compiling activist security guides — commonly point toward VPN providers with an independently audited no-logs policy, rather than free or ad-supported apps of unknown origin.

What to check

Install it before you need it — some VPN provider websites are themselves blocked once you're in-country. Set it to connect automatically rather than relying on remembering to turn it on, and confirm the kill switch is enabled so traffic doesn't leak if the VPN connection drops.
