First Aid

Bleeding Control

Direct pressure, wound packing, and when a tourniquet is appropriate — based on Red Cross first aid guidance.

Immediate steps

Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound with the cleanest material available and keep it in place — don't lift it to check. For a wound that won't stop with pressure alone, packing the wound before applying pressure is the next step.

Response by severity — general guidance, not a substitute for certified training
SituationFirst response
Minor bleedingDirect pressure with clean cloth until it stops
Won't stop with pressurePack the wound, then reapply firm pressure
Severe limb bleeding, uncontrolledApply a tourniquet above the wound; note the time applied
Object embedded in woundDo not remove it; pack dressings around it and bandage in place

When to escalate

A tourniquet is for severe limb bleeding that direct pressure isn't controlling. Note the time it was applied — that detail matters for the medical team that treats the person next.
